**Course:** CPE 334 - Introduction to Software Engineering in the Age of AI Agents

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This SRS defines EventCheck's observable product behavior, business constraints, quality targets, data ownership, and planned sprint allocation. It provides a baseline for the two student developers, teammate reviewers, instructor/TA, and acceptance testers. The Feature Inventory and Requirements Traceability Matrix are maintained as separate engineering documents. Architecture, database design, permission matrix, and authentication mechanisms are specified in the System-Level SDS. Feature contracts refine requirements into Acceptance Criteria (AC) and Software Test Specifications (STS) before implementation.

Requirements use stable FR, BR, and NFR identifiers. **Must** means required for the stated product increment, **Should** means desirable with an explicitly documented reason needed for deferral, and **Could** means optional. Priority does not imply instructor approval of a proposed policy. Course requirements are attributed to [R1] and [R2]; unresolved product refinements and quantitative targets remain proposals pending review. A proposed requirement expresses the recommended baseline, not a confirmed stakeholder decision. Revisions must record their rationale and preserve IDs; downstream contracts must resolve relevant open questions before implementation.

### 1.2 Product Overview

EventCheck is a university event registration and attendance platform. Organizers publish university events with limited capacity and retrieve registrations for their own events. Participants discover published events, register, and retrieve their saved registrations. Example contexts include workshops, seminars, and student activities. Later increments add cancellation, deadlines, capacity changes, check-in, and attendance summaries.

The product shall provide a real UI-to-application/API-to-persistent-database workflow. A static interface, generic CRUD demonstration, or browser-only storage does not satisfy the baseline [R1, Section 5; R2, Section 2]. Visitors may browse published events and public details without logging in under agreed Q-08; registration and all private or management operations require authentication.

### 1.3 Stakeholder Problem

The motivating product hypothesis is that manual forms and disconnected lists make it difficult to determine available places, prevent duplicate registrations and overbooking, retrieve registration confirmations, and reconcile attendance. Organizers need a consistent authorized registration list; participants need an understandable registration outcome and access to their own records. These are proposed stakeholder problems, not findings from interviews or evidence of stakeholder approval.

### 1.4 Product Goals

1. Centralize event publication, discovery, and persisted registration in one workflow.
2. Prevent duplicate active registrations and overbooking, including competing requests for the last place.
3. Restrict private registrations and management operations by authenticated role and record ownership.
4. Make registration outcomes and remaining capacity visible without exposing participant lists to other participants.
5. Establish reliable, authorized attendance records and summaries in later sprints.
6. Deliver increments that can be independently reproduced and verified using synthetic data.

### 1.5 Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Participant | Authenticated user role permitted to register and access its own registrations. |
| Organizer | Authenticated user role permitted to publish and manage its owned events and their registration lists. |
| Event | A scheduled activity with descriptive details, one owning Organizer, publication state, and positive capacity. |
| Published Event | An event made visible in event discovery by its owner after validation. Publication does not guarantee that a place remains. |
| Capacity | Maximum number of active registrations allowed for one event. |
| Registration | Persisted association between one Participant and one event, including identity, creation time, and lifecycle state. |
| Active Registration | A confirmed, non-cancelled registration that consumes one place; attendance does not release that place. |
| Eligible Event | Published event satisfying the registration conditions applicable to its sprint, including available capacity and, from Sprint 2, the proposed deadline rule. |
| Check-in | Authorized action recording an eligible registrant's attendance at an event. |
| Attendance | Persisted evidence that an active registrant was checked in, rather than an inference from registration alone. |
| Owner | Organizer account associated with an event; ownership grants access only within that event's management scope. |
| Sprint | Planned product increment; later-sprint allocation below is proposed by the team unless explicitly mandated by the course. |

## 2. Stakeholders and User Roles

| Actor | Description | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participant | Person seeking a place at a published event. | Browse event details; register; retrieve own registrations; later cancel and view own attendance. |
| Organizer | Person responsible for events belonging to their account. | Create and publish owned events; set capacity; view owned event registrations; later manage deadlines/capacity, record attendance, and view summaries. |

The two student engineers maintain and verify the product; the instructor/TA evaluates scope and evidence; peer testers provide UAT feedback. These stakeholders are not additional application roles. Unauthenticated visitors are an access state, not a third role.

Backend authorization shall enforce role and ownership on every protected operation, regardless of visible UI controls or client-supplied identifiers. Participants may read only their own private registrations. An Organizer may read registrant details only for owned events and cannot manage another Organizer's event. Ownership is established from authenticated identity at creation, not granted by a client-selected owner. Broader permissions, role switching, and multi-organizer collaboration are not approved. Under agreed Q-08, unauthenticated visitors may list published events and view public details, capacity, and remaining places, but never registrant identities or private lists. Unpublished events remain private to their owners.

## 3. Product Scope

### 3.1 In Scope

The complete product includes login, logout, current-user identity, agreed server-side session authentication with Secure, HttpOnly cookies, server-side role/ownership authorization, public event discovery and capacity display, event creation and publication, persisted registration, personal registration retrieval, and organizer retrieval of owned event registration lists. It also includes registration cancellation, deadline enforcement, controlled capacity modification, organizer-operated check-in, attendance retrieval, and event-level registration/attendance reporting in later increments. Remaining policy details are recorded in Sections 8-9.

### 3.2 Out of Scope

The following are proposed team scope exclusions, not instructor prohibitions: online payments and paid tickets; email/SMS delivery; third-party identity providers; self-service account creation, recovery, and email verification; advanced account administration; recommendation algorithms; waiting lists; multi-organizer collaboration or ownership transfer; QR/scanner integrations; report export; platform-wide analytics; and Organizer-initiated event cancellation. Public discovery remains in scope under agreed Q-08, while registration and private or management operations require authentication. External integrations are not required by [R1]. Adding any excluded capability requires an explicit scope revision and impact review.

### 3.3 Sprint Boundaries

| Sprint | Planned outcome and boundary |

Both students remain responsible for requirements, tests, implementation review, and delivery rather than holding permanent documentation/code roles [R1, Section 2].

Persisted after acceptance. | Owning Organizer manages event attendance; Participant reads only attendance associated with own registration. | At most one record per registration; event association must agree; eligible active registration and authorized recording actor required. |

Active registrations consume places regardless of attendance. Cancelled registrations do not consume places and remain retrievable under the proposed lifecycle. Registration and attendance lists, credentials, and unrelated personal information must never appear in public discovery responses. Timestamps are persisted as UTC-based instants and initially displayed/input as Asia/Bangkok (UTC+7), clearly labelled. A missing attendance record means no check-in recorded, not a positive attendance claim. No automatic deletion or retention-management interface is in this baseline; synthetic registration and attendance history is retained for the project and assessment period, while any real-user production policy remains to be defined under Q-10.
